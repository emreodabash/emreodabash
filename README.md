### A little about how I think

Mechanics makes me ask **what moves**.  
Electronics makes me ask **what changes**.  
Software makes me ask **what I forgot to consider**.

I'm interested in the point where these questions meet: systems that sense, decide, and act in the physical world.

I'm still building my foundations. I would rather understand a simple circuit thoroughly than claim to have mastered a complex system I can't explain.

### Tools, not trophies

`C` · `Python` · `KiCad` · `Git` · `GitHub` · `VS Code`

I care less about collecting tool logos and more about understanding what each tool makes possible.

### My preferred algorithm

```text
observe → question → model → build → measure → rethink
                                              ↳ repeat
```

Three things I try to remember:

- **A working prototype is evidence, not an explanation.**
- **A failed experiment with good notes is not a wasted experiment.**
- **If the assumptions are wrong, elegant code won't save the system.**

### Beyond the workbench

I've been involved in technology education mentoring and volunteer work at technology events. They taught me something useful: a good idea becomes more valuable when you can explain it, challenge it, and build it with other people.

I want this profile to grow into a collection of small, reproducible engineering case files—not just polished outcomes, but the reasoning and experiments behind them.
